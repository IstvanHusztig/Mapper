## 3. Fix

Decision (user, 2026-10-08): the deeper rework (option 2), because dMapper is meant for surveying large caves (hundreds of metres), where blind passages are common and precision is the requirement.

Mechanism, from the code: with the accelerometer term off, `optimize_lidar_odometry` already has a strong velocity-continuity prior (about 1 mm/s per node step, about 2 cm/s per window), yet the windows of the blind stretch lost 0.15 to 0.4 m/s. What pulls them back is the NDT map itself: along a passage the small Gaussians hold the points where earlier scans put them (`docs/degeneracy-information.html`: the NDT information looks strong even in a corridor), so the registration drags along the blind axis, and the accelerometer gate only decides which tie-break wins. The established remedy is degeneracy-aware optimization (Zhang, Kaess, Singh 2016): along directions the geometry does not constrain, the LiDAR must not move the solution; the motion model (velocity, refined by the accelerometer) decides there.

- [x] 3.1 Loop closure that can't wreck a run: the pose graph of `run_loop_closure` gets Levenberg-Marquardt damping (a step is kept only if the cost falls) and a plausibility check of its result (all finite, and no segment moved more than `loop_closure_max_correction_m` plus the largest accepted edge correction; otherwise the result is discarded with a warning and the trajectory stays as it was). Tests on a small synthetic pose graph (consistent loops are applied, a contradicting loop set is refused), without the 20 s cases. Verify: `ctest`; `44_short` IDENTICAL (no loops there) and the full 44 with the shipped settings unchanged or within 1e-6 if the damping does not change converged results. Commit.

  Result: `optimize_loop_pose_graph` (Levenberg-Marquardt: the plain Gauss-Newton step is kept when it doesn't raise the weighted squared error, otherwise the step is damped and repeated; the angle residuals of the cost are wrapped into (-pi, pi], found by the new test with a 180 deg turn) and `loop_pose_graph_plausible` (finite, no segment moved further than `loop_closure_max_correction_m` plus the largest accepted loop correction), both declared in `loop_closure.h` with `LoopClosureEdge`; an implausible result is discarded with a warning and the trajectory stays unchanged. Tests (fast, not skipped): a square of 8 segments drifting 5 cm per segment is closed by one exact loop edge (the last segment's error falls below 20 %) and passes the check; a 1 m move passes, a 2.2e6 m move and a NaN are refused. `ctest` passes (Release); `44_short` and the full 44 with the shipped settings have identical data files (only the parameter file differs, by the new setting lines), loop closure 20 loops, largest segment correction 0.51 m as before.
Research (2026-10-08, `research.md` in this folder): the literature agrees that hard switches flip (Zhang 2016 remapping, dMapper's gate), that soft, per-direction treatment works best (Tuna et al. 2024; Hatleskog & Alexis 2025, who also show that noise adds spurious information along the blind axis), and that LiDAR-inertial systems that work in tunnels and caves keep the IMU always on (Wildcat, LIO-SAM, FAST-LIO2). For NDT specifically, a flat 0.15 m cell also penalizes sliding along its plane (in-plane sigma about 4 cm), which is false stiffness along a passage, and partly filled cells at the map frontier pull backwards: the drag seen here. Order of the remaining steps, each measured on its own:

Computed once per cell where the inverse is stored (`refresh_cell_inverse`, task 2.1 of `ndt-lookup-cache`), so it costs nothing per point; the setting reaches every `update_rgd` call of the odometry map. Tests: a planar cell's regularized inverse has the old information along the normal and at most 1 / sigma² in the plane; a non-planar cell is unchanged; with the setting off everything is bit-identical (`44_short` IDENTICAL).
