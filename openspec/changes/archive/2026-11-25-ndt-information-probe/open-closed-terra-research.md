# Open/closed geometry: focused research note

2026-11-13. Research only; no implementation or processing runs. This is an independent literature survey for the primary agent, not an independent review of a causal conclusion. Recommendations are hypotheses, not evidence that the outdoor failure has a particular cause.

## Evidence and scope

The current experimental Huber3, alpha 0.1/beta 0.1, online gyro bias Livox2 full run returned about 62 m off. Cauchy3 on all cell residuals reduced window 415's local turn from 9.19° to 1.64° but the full return worsened to about 114 m and minimum Z to -49 m. The new Livox3 indoor run with the frozen Huber configuration returned to the same table/direction with 5.6 cm pre-loop and 11 cm final endpoint discrepancy, no >3° bends, and max speed 1.3 m/s. Its selected residual chi-square median 32 and p95 314 (377 selected) do not establish calibrated noise or correct correspondences. Livox3 changed both route and recorder/capture/confidence filtering; this comparison does not isolate geometry, hardware, or preprocessing.

In the scratch NDT mapper, residuals use each matched cell's inverse covariance. Legacy means and view-gated covariance, potentially only millimetres wide, can make a cell appear very informative; this is geometric spread, not automatically a calibrated measurement uncertainty. Existing projection in `scan_to_map.cpp` uses a 3D sum of unit-normal outer products and relative eigenvalue ratios, and scales translation while retaining rotation. The sum is not weighted by Huber or covariance and can count a point in both grids; it is not a calibrated full pose/yaw information measure. `ndt_window_cost` is reassociated and matched-only, so losing correspondences can make a candidate look cheaper. The diagnostic trace explicitly says its full objective is incomplete. Velocity and gyro-bias states are carried across windows. The W415 local cause audit establishes thin-cell dominance for that local correction: three coarse cells had narrow widths 5.25, 10.36 and 7.71 mm and normal residuals 0.148, 0.188 and 0.240 m; their net angular RHS·step contribution was 171.88 of 183.60. In the exact 100-iteration control, ablating these three cells reduced the turn from 9.14° to 2.66°. This local causal result does not establish the cause of the later full-route failure.

## What the primary sources actually support

1. **Cell uncertainty and scale.** PCL's official [voxel covariance implementation](https://pointclouds.org/documentation/voxel__grid__covariance_8hpp_source.html) floors small covariance eigenvalues as a fraction of the largest to avoid near-singular cells, after requiring a minimum population. Its [documented defaults](https://pointclouds.org/documentation/classpcl_1_1_voxel_grid_covariance.html) are six points and 0.01 eigenvalue ratio; these are numerical regularizers, not calibrated Livox uncertainties or recommended dMapper values. A physical floor on residual variance, informed by repeat scans and deskew/map uncertainty, would bound the influence of very thin cells while retaining their plane normal; changing covariance also changes Mahalanobis robust weights and the relative IMU/LiDAR balance. A floor cannot repair a wrong cell mean, mixed vegetation, dynamic points, or poor associations. Relative inflation can also blur useful cave walls and depends on voxel extent. Test physical and relative alternatives separately, not by copying PCL defaults.

2. **Weak geometry is directional, and distinct from bad matches.** [X-ICP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16335) analyzes scan-map correspondences against pose directions and constrains updates where geometry is weak. It assumes sufficiently meaningful correspondences and a useful motion/initial estimate; frozen weak directions still drift with that estimate and cannot supply unobserved motion. A long featureless cave passage can lack axial information; a large chamber can have sparse/distant support and poor overlap rather than the same degeneracy. Translation-only ratios miss coupled yaw/translation, while raw full 6D Hessian eigenvalues mix metres and radians and depend on origin, range, count and weighting. Use a stated reference point and length scale, and inspect yaw and coupled directions, spatial/range distribution and absolute effective support as well as normalized ratios. A high eigenvalue or low residual is not proof that associations are correct. This is diagnostic first, not a recommendation to switch on stronger projection.

3. **Match quality and acceptance.** The official [FAST-LIO2 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.06829) uses direct raw-point registration with an iterated tightly coupled filter and an incremental map; its [implementation](https://github.com/hku-mars/FAST_LIO/blob/main/src/laserMapping.cpp) requires nearest-neighbor support, a locally valid plane and a residual-based selection before contributing a match. This illustrates separating support/fit from the solver; it does not validate transplanting its thresholds or plane model to NDT. [KISS-ICP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15397) combines adaptive correspondence thresholding and a robust kernel in point-to-point ICP, a different objective and no IMU; it does not establish that adapting a threshold based on a failing estimate is safe. Changing match count or a redescending kernel can hide failure by discarding hard points. A comparable candidate-step check must account for fixed support or explicit unmatched penalties, frozen versus reassociated cells, and *all* prior terms. It should compare true objective change and predicted reduction, plus overlap/effective count; neither the existing matched-only cost nor a small incremental step alone is sufficient. Repeatedly small accepted corrections can accumulate a large heading error.

4. **Priors can coast or run away.** IMU and velocity estimates provide continuity, not absolute position or yaw; bias and integration errors accumulate in weak geometry. Reliable constraints can reduce drift but overconfident or contaminated carried states can propagate it. The evidence does not yet say whether a bad prior drove LiDAR, bad LiDAR contaminated the prior, or both. Log separate factor contributions, prior innovation/covariance and accumulated LiDAR-versus-gyro relative rotation over a window and over successive windows. Check whether the proposed step improves fixed-support LiDAR fit while producing a physically plausible inertial innovation; gyro bias and real rotation uncertainty must be included. A hard angular clamp risks rejecting legitimate turns and hiding failure, especially in confined passages. On insufficient support, reporting uncertainty/failure and avoiding map contamination may be safer than silently asserting a pose; indefinite IMU-only propagation is not a recovery strategy.

## Ranked smallest plausible next interventions

1. **Discriminate the later sustained failure before extrapolating the local result.** W415 and W754 are known local gates; use existing data to examine the later W904–916 Huber regime and the approximately W908 Cauchy regime, where trace coverage remains a gap. Compare Huber and Cauchy only where incoming state/map and support permit a meaningful comparison. Record cell population, eigenwidths, ranges, effective weighted counts, signed residual contributions to yaw and translation, pre/post fixed-association cost and reassociated overlap, LiDAR/IMU/bias/velocity factor contributions, and cumulative heading correction. A frozen-map, same-support perturbation profile distinguishes a local fit/optimization issue from loss of support; it cannot validate a self-contaminated map. Determine whether the later regime also shows thin-cell dominance, support collapse, weak yaw, or carried-state dominance. Use existing traces where adequate; no assertion that this has been measured.
2. **Test one physically motivated variance floor as a local candidate** in the residual information, leaving map statistics and all other settings fixed. Treat the floor as an uncertainty model hypothesis, not a magic threshold, and account for changed robust scale. Compare signed influence, support and gyro innovation before/after; a better local W415 turn is insufficient. Cell mean accuracy and vegetation remain independent concerns. W415 justifies this local candidate; derive its floor from measurement and map uncertainty, not outcome tuning, and do not assume it fixes the later regime.
3. **If the evidence instead shows step/association failure, test a minimal support-aware trust/acceptance rule** using a complete, consistently evaluated objective and explicit support protection, then protect map/state carry on rejected or unsupported windows. Alternatively, if weak *rotational* geometry is demonstrated with reliable matches, explore a direction-aware constraint with the IMU uncertainty and report resulting drift. These are distinct branches, not a bundle. No evidence currently justifies a global Cauchy replacement, environment classifier, broad prior retune, or arbitrary angular clamp.

## Proposed evaluation gate (future authorized work)

All new runs require user approval. First specify the later outdoor failure clip boundaries from the existing audit and preserve pre-onset context and incoming map/state; do not start it cold at W415. A 20–30 second clip with carried state is useful only if it reproduces the established control, including feedback. Apply each single candidate against the frozen Huber baseline with identical recording, settings, boundaries and initialization. Inspect the whole clip, not one window: heading against gyro with bias uncertainty, cumulative corrections, support/overlap, effective weighted count, cell widths, factor innovations, speed, vertical drift and trajectory/map continuity. Failure to improve the identified onset or loss of support stops the candidate. Then run the surviving candidate on the full Livox2 route and compare return and map geometry, and on the frozen Livox3 indoor route to protect its trajectory, returns, walls and floors. Evaluate cloud and logs under the project verification contracts; compare candidate differences with robustness/noise-floor measurements before ranking. An indoor endpoint alone and any single local turn are inadequate. If recorder or IMU quality is in doubt, verify each recording before attributing differences to scene geometry. No arbitrary nine-run broad parameter search: only test a predeclared alternative when the diagnostic discriminates it, and disclose thresholds and runtime.

## Unresolved questions

What causes the later sustained W904–916 Huber / approximately W908 Cauchy failure, given the trace gap, and does the W415 thin-cell mechanism recur there? Are small covariance widths and residuals repeatable across visits and capture changes? Does support actually decline, or is it concentrated in wrong/moving cells? Does the gyro/bias and velocity carry precede or follow the first discrepancy? What reference geometry exists for the failure clip, and what are the allowed noise-floor tolerances for indoor preservation? These answers decide which branch, if any, deserves a code test.
