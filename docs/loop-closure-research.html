Loop closure: diagnosis and practice

Research notes for change G (openspec/changes/loop-closure-fixes), October 2026. What dMapper's loop closure does, why its result on recording 44 is fragile, what established LiDAR SLAM systems do, and what follows for the fixes C22–C25 of docs/code-review-tracker.md. In short Loop closure on 44 never compares the gallery's way out with its way back, so the gallery's doubled walls stay and its angle is set by how the pose graph happens to spread other loops' errors. Letting those revisits in makes the gallery consistent and the results less fragile, but it shows that the façade angle came out right in two runs only by chance: the gallery keeps the odometry's −4° unless the drift itself shrinks or a constraint ties the gallery to the start.

2. Diagnosis on recording 44

2.1 The gallery never gets a loop

State C10 (master 2026-10-14), inputs s0–s5 (44 with 0–5 leading IMU samples dropped): 31–39 loops accepted per run (≈ 20 on the same floor, ≈ 15 between floors), 1–5 dropped as outliers; the counts don't differ between passing and failing runs. None of them touches the gallery (segments 9–14, 44–70 s). The gallery is a dead end walked out (≈ 45–58 s) and back (≈ 59–70 s); its out and back segments are 10–25 s apart, under the 30 s gap. The loops jump from the corridor before it (segment 7, 34 s) to the corridor after it (segment 15, 73 s). So:

  • the gallery's doubled walls (out against back) are left to the odometry;
  • its heading is set by how the pose graph spreads the cycle error of 7→15 over segments 8–14: evenly, because all odometry edges are equal, not where the drift happened;
  • the loops at the end of the recording (start room ↔ return, 218–280 s) correct 2.9 m in s4 but 0.7 m in s1: different odometry drift, spread to different places, which fits the reshuffled façade results across s0–s5.

Better The gallery's way out and way back agree within 0.1° in every run (C10: up to 1.84°); more revisit pairs register (7–10 of 11 instead of 2–6) and their median heading error halves; the façade spread shrinks from 3.7° to 2.3°: less fragile.
Worse The gallery against the start is −2.6 … −5.0° in every run, about the odometry's drift: no run passes (C10: 2 of 6). The stairwell offsets grow somewhat (s0 10.8 → 14.3 cm, s4 14.9 → 19.3 cm).

Reading (inference, to be checked): C10's two "passing" runs were luck: without gallery loops, the cycle error of 7→15 was spread over the gallery's segments and happened to turn them towards the right angle in some runs. With the gallery tied to itself, its heading follows the junction, which carries the drift accumulated on the way there. Loop closure on 44 has no loop that ties the gallery's direction to the start, so the global angle can only come from less odometry drift (changes F/E) or a new constraint between start and gallery.

3. Practice in established systems

3.1 Candidate gating

  • Time gap: LIO-SAM (15 m radius, historyKeyframeSearchTimeDiff = 30 s). The odd one out: it rejects short out-and-back trips and admits standing still.
  • Path or index gap: SC-PGO (FAST_LIO_SLAM) excludes recent keyframes by index, and its header notes that a distance-based exclusion works as well; hdl_graph_slam requires a travelled path (accum_distance_thresh: 8 m in the code default, 25 m in the launch file), an XY radius of 15 m and 15 m of path between accepted loops; LAMP 2.0 (mines and caves) scales its search radius with the path, dmax = 0.2 m × node-index gap with nodes every 2 m (≈ 10 % of the path).
  • No gap, overlap only: GLIM (every submap pair within 100 m with overlap ≥ 0.2, neighbours included); KISS-SLAM (all earlier local maps split by distance, 40 % overlap).

3.2 Loop edge weighting (practice disagrees)

  • Fixed or scalar: Cartographer (translation weight 1.1e4, rotation 1e5); SC-PGO (variance 0.5 with a Cauchy kernel); LIO-SAM (isotropic variance = ICP fitness); hdl_graph_slam (fitness mapped to an isotropic sigma, clamped to 0.1–5 m and 0.05–0.2 rad).
  • From the registration: MULLS (I = AᵀPA / σ̂², the Hessian over the posterior residual variance); GLIM (GICP Hessian for between factors, the full VGICP cost as its loop factor); HBA (bundle-adjustment Hessian).
  • Pitfalls: closed-form ICP covariances are over-optimistic (Bonnabel 2016); their error comes mostly from initialization and convergence, not sensor noise (Brossard 2020); points are not independent, so the raw Hessian gives millimetre sigmas; degenerate directions need care (Hinduja 2019 constrains only well-conditioned directions; DARE-SLAM keeps degenerate scans out of the loop search; LAMP 2.0 avoids closing loops in degenerate areas).

3.3 Odometry edge covariances

  • LIO-SAM and SC-PGO: fixed per keyframe (1 cm, 0.057°), but keyframes come every 1–2 m or 0.2 rad, so the covariance effectively grows with distance, not time. Cartographer: fixed weights per node, nodes thinned by a motion filter. GLIM: IMU preintegration plus registration Hessians. HBA: BA Hessians.
  • Least squares spreads a loop's residual along the cycle in proportion to each edge's covariance. With fixed sigmas per 4.8 s, a stationary segment absorbs as much correction as a walking one, and a degenerate one no more than a well-constrained one (inference).

3.4 Outliers (20–40 loops)

  • No kernel: LIO-SAM (relies on its gates). Huber: hdl_graph_slam, Cartographer (scale 10); convex, so one bad loop still pulls. Cauchy: SC-PGO. DCS and switchable constraints in older robust back-ends.
  • PCM (pairwise consistency maximization, Mangelson 2018; Kimera-RPGO): the maximum clique of pairwise-consistent loops. LAMP adds an odometry-cycle check (reject when the mean error per edge exceeds 0.1 m or 0.05 rad).
  • GNC-TLS (Yang 2020): no initial guess needed, tolerates 70–80 % outliers; Kimera2 found it better than PCM on most sequences, worse on some; LAMP 2.0 runs a PCM-style prefilter, then GNC.
  • PCM's χ² test needs honest covariances; LAMP fell back to fixed thresholds without them.

3.5 Anchors and dense correction

  • GLIM: submap origin at the centre frame. LIO-SAM / SC-PGO: target = the candidate keyframe ±25 keyframes, centred on it.
  • Cartographer, LIO-SAM: every node or keyframe is in the graph (no interpolation). KISS-SLAM: a second PGO over the dense local trajectories with the keyposes fixed. HBA: top-down PGO over all frames. MULLS: each submap's reference frame fixed, frames inside optimised.
  • A start anchor couples a rotation error into translation over the lever arm to the points; a diagonal fixed covariance ignores that coupling (inference).

4. What follows for change G

In order; each step measured from the snapshots on s0–s5 against the current noise floor before the next one, with an acceptance rule written before its runs (openspec/changes/loop-closure-fixes/design.md):

  1. G1, revisits by path (C22): ≥ 8 m walked between the two segments and centre distance ≤ min(8 m, 0.5 × path); the time gap goes. Expected from the 8 s experiment: a consistent gallery and less fragility, while the façade angle stays at the odometry's drift.
  2. G2, candidate choice and redundancy (C24): the best passing registration instead of the first nearest; 2–3 loop edges per segment. Expected: fewer discrete flips between near-identical inputs (inference).
  3. G3, honest weights (C23): odometry edges with translation variance σ0² + kd·distance and rotation variance σr0² + kt·Δt + kψ·|Δψ|, inflated along the windows' weak directions; same-floor loop edges from the registration information H / σ̂², inflated by a factor calibrated from repeated registrations from perturbed starts, floored (≈ 2 cm, 0.1°), near zero along weak directions; k values calibrated from accepted loops' residuals against path length.
  4. G4, outliers (C24): odometry-cycle and pairwise consistency checks (an exact maximum clique is cheap for ≈ 40 loops), then GNC-TLS with the odometry edges as known inliers, replacing "least squares, then kernel, then drop the worst edge". Needs G3.
  5. G5, anchors (C25): anchor at the segment's middle node (or point centroid); the correction onto the nodes by path length, or a second small pose graph with the anchors fixed.
  6. Later, if doubled walls remain after the pose graph: plane bundle adjustment (BALM2 / HBA) as a final refinement (precision first, costs time).
Open: the global angle Loop closure can make places consistent, not straight. The façade angle needs either less odometry drift (the map model, changes F/E: cell statistics C20, coarse grid C18) or a loop between start and gallery. A candidate in the data: the façade is seen through the start room's windows, about 10 m from the gallery, outside today's 8 m search radius; whether a registration between those views holds is to be checked.
