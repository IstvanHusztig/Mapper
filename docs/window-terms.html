The per-term table: what it is and how to read it

A window's result is where about ten terms (the LiDAR against the map, the gyro, the accelerometer, the priors) agree. The per-term table takes them apart: for each term on its own, how strongly it holds the window and where it would move it. It tells which term decides a window, which one is overruled, and where the terms disagree. This page explains the table and then reads it on a real stretch: 30 seconds of an indoor walk. The reading gives no verdict on the map and proposes no fix.

Terms used here: window, guess, term, residual, sigma and information, cost, prior, unknowns, information ratio, per-term table, bend, velocity chi². All basic terms: the guide's Fundamentals.

What the table is

Each term of a window's optimization adds its equations to one least-squares problem (its part of the normal matrix H and the right-hand side b). The table builds each term's equations on their own, with the same term functions the solver calls, and asks of each one: if this term were alone, how firmly would it hold the window, and where would it move it?

It does not ask this of every unknown (a window has more than a thousand), but of twelve motions a person can picture: four kinds of motion of the whole window (below), each along the direction of travel, sideways and vertical. For a motion m (a vector over the window's unknowns, at an amplitude of 1 m or 1°) a term's weight is mᵀHm: the curvature of its cost along that motion. Its hold is 1/√weight, a sigma in metres or degrees. Its pull is mᵀb / weight: how far along that motion its own minimum lies.

  • Rows: one per term (the fine and the coarse NDT grid, the NDT before the degeneracy projection, the edges' position and rotation rows, the gyro-bias prior, the velocity continuity, the accelerometer, the velocity prior, zero velocity, the end-velocity tie, the acceleration-offset prior, the origin fix), plus a total row (all terms together) and a damping row (what the solver's damping adds).
  • Twice per window: at the guess (before the first iteration) and at the result (after the last).
  • Also per term: its cost and number of residuals.

How to get it. Set log_window_terms = true in [lidar_odometry_debug_info]. Every window then logs one window terms N line, and a run writes window_terms.csv (every row of every window) and window_features.csv (one line per window: its readings, its verdict, its noise model) to the output folder; a replay writes them next to the capture. python tools/window_terms.py <file>_window_terms.csv prints a summary of a range, and with --window N one window's table. The table changes no result; a replay takes about 9 % longer with it.

Its limit. A pull is one linear step with everything else held still. It shows where a term pulls, not where the window would end if that term were changed (see "How far the table can be trusted" below).

How to read the table

For every window the optimizer adds up about ten terms (LiDAR against the map, gyro, accelerometer, priors) and solves for the window's poses. The table takes each term on its own and asks two questions about four motions of the window.

MotionWhat movesAmplitude
shiftevery node by the same vectormetres
stretchthe window travels further or shorter (node k by a share of its time in the window)metres at the last node
curvethe window accelerates (the share squared)metres at the last node
turnthe attitude turns progressively over the window, positions unchangeddegrees at the last node

Each motion is taken along travel, sideways and vertical (for turn: the rotation axis, so "turn vertical" is a heading bend).

  • Hold is a sigma: the term alone keeps that motion to ± hold. A smaller hold is a stronger term. Weight is 1 / hold², and weights add up, so shares of the total weight say who decides.
  • Pull is where the term's own minimum lies from the present state, with everything else held.
  • Remaining step is the pull of all terms together. At the result it is near zero when the terms balance, and large when the iterations ended before the balance was reached.
  • Cost per residual is the term's cost divided by its number of residuals, measured with its own weight. It is about 1 only where that weight is a noise model.

Each row exists at the guess (before the first iteration) and at the result (after the last).

What the example shows

1. Along the twelve motions, every window ended in balance

At the result the remaining step is 0.0000 m for shift, stretch and curve in all three directions in every window (largest 0.009 mm), and at most 0.0002° for turn. So along these twelve motions no window was stopped by the damping before its terms balanced: there the result is the point where the terms agree, and the weights decide it. The table measures only these twelve motions, not every unknown of a window.

No window ended because it had converged: all 84 ran the full 100 iterations, and by then the damping holds every motion to about 0.002 mm, so the late iterations hardly move a window. The balance was reached before that.

The gyro's hold is nearly the same in every window. It is the configured value: the gyro's angle random walk 0.057 °/√s × the inflation factor 7.09 × √0.358 s = 0.242°. The factor 7.09 is a tuned setting, not the sensor's own noise; without it the hold would be 0.034°. The LiDAR terms outweigh the gyro rows about 800 to 1.

What the windows did: from guess to result a window's heading bend changed by a median 0.118° (90 % of the windows below 0.30°, largest 0.43°). Over 0.358 s that is 0.33 °/s, 0.83 °/s and 1.19 °/s. The gyro rows accept this: their cost per residual at the result is median 0.29, at most 1.02. For roll and pitch the picture is the same (gyro share 0.29 % and 0.24 %; the windows' tilt changed by a median 0.06° and 0.11°, at most 0.31° and 0.58°).

4. The start velocity ends a median 2.9 sigmas from its prior

The velocity prior says: this window starts with the velocity the previous one ended with. Its sigma along travel is 4.6 mm/s (median). After the solve its cost per residual is median 8.65, for 90 % of the windows below 30.8, at most 65.4. As distances (rms over its three residuals) that is 2.9, 5.5 and 8.1 sigmas. It is above 1 in 81 of 84 windows.

The largest values are spread over the stretch (windows 358, 338, 316, 303 and 302: 65, 50, 43, 41 and 40), not tied to the weak geometry of point 6. The table shows the distance, not its cause: the prior may be too tight, or the solved velocity may be off.

5. The result lies to one side of the guess

From guess to result the window's sideways stretch is negative in 65 of 84 windows (median −2.7 mm, mean −3.4 mm over 0.358 s), and the sideways curve in 63 of 84. At the result the edge positions and the velocity prior pull the other way (median +4.2 mm and +2.7 mm). Along travel there is no such preference (47 of 84 positive).

6. The eight windows with weak geometry (110.9–113.8 s)

In windows 309 to 317 the information ratio falls to 0.117–0.170, and the degeneracy projection takes away part of the LiDAR's weight along travel. Over the whole stretch it does so in 12 windows, in 5 of them more than half (311 to 315). Three more short dips in the information ratio (window 301 and 328 to 330, 0.17–0.20) lose little or nothing.

  • In six windows in a row (311 to 316) the window ends travelling further than its guess: +3.0, +10.0, +8.1, +4.7, +11.8 and +30.0 mm. In each of them the velocity prior pulls back (−4.4 to −20.3 mm).
  • When the geometry returns (window 316: 58 % kept, the LiDAR back at 88 % of the weight) the window moves 30 mm away from its guess in one step, 6.6 sigmas from its velocity prior, and the next window moves 12.7 mm back.

7. The two grids disagree by centimetres in a few windows

The indoor and the outdoor grid's pulls for the travel distance differ by a median 1.6 mm (90 % of the windows below 5.4 mm). The largest differences are in windows 312 (25 mm: outdoor +27.0, indoor +1.8), 350 (16 mm: outdoor −14.2, indoor +1.8), 313 (15 mm) and 351 (9 mm). Windows 312 and 313 have weak geometry; 350 and 351 have not (information ratio 0.38 and 0.32).

8. At the guess, the accelerometer row is very large

At the guess the accelerometer's cost per residual is median 359 (at most 3980); at the result it is 0.114. The guess path itself is integrated from the accelerometer, so the path alone should not cost that much.

One start value fits the size: prepare_velocity_state sets the window's end velocity to the start velocity, while the guess path accelerates. The end-velocity term would then start with a residual equal to the window's change of velocity (median 0.2 m/s), and its weight is between 0.4 and 4 million per (m/s)². The measured ratio of guess cost to squared velocity change is 1.05 million (median; correlation 0.69). This is consistent with the start value being the cause, but not shown: the row lumps the acceleration terms and the start and end velocity terms, so the table can't separate them.

Consequence for reading the table: at the guess the accelerometer's pulls (for example 16° of tilt in a tenth of the windows) should not be read as a statement about the path.

How far the table can be trusted

A pull is one linear step with everything else held. To see what that costs, the table at the guess can be compared with what each window then actually did (84 windows):

MotionCorrelation, along / sideways / verticalActual change per predicted step
stretch0.60 / 0.83 / 0.760.65 / 0.75 / 0.75
turn0.48 / 0.63 / 0.580.40 / 0.49 / 0.44
curve0.27 / 0.51 / −0.090.05 / 0.11 / −0.03

For stretch the table at the guess gives the direction and about two thirds of the size of what a window does, for turn about half. For curve it gives little at the guess (only sideways shows a relation), which fits the large accelerometer row there (point 8). The result rows are the ones to read. Two cautions: neighbouring windows are not independent, so 84 windows are less evidence than the count suggests; and shift can't be tested this way, because the first node never moves.

What the table can't say

  • Which side is right. Where the LiDAR and the IMU terms disagree (points 3, 5 and 6) the table shows the size of the disagreement and who wins it. It has no reference to say who should.
  • Whether the windows of point 6 lost distance. The table shows the mechanism at work there (the projection cuts the LiDAR, the prior holds, the window still ends beyond its guess six times in a row, then a jump). Whether the path came out short needs a reference, such as the plan or a second pass.
  • Anything about another recording. This stretch comes from a recording whose map was judged good. A stretch that goes wrong needs its own capture and its own reading.

How to make such a reading

Capture a stretch of a run (capture_windows_from_s / capture_windows_to_s), then replay it with the table on:

python tools/run_replay.py <window_capture_<from>-<to>s.bin> <settings with log_window_terms = true>
python tools/window_terms.py <params name>_window_terms.csv                # per-term summary of the range
python tools/window_terms.py <params name>_window_terms.csv --window 313   # one window's table

The example was read on 2026-11-17 (code at commit 28952e4); its numbers were recomputed independently by a reviewer before the page was written.
