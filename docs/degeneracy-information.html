Degeneracy information: when the LiDAR can't see where you moved

What the "LiDAR translation information" is, how dMapper uses it to decide when to trust the accelerometer, how to read its log line, and a known weakness found in October 2026. It is computed in optimize_lidar_odometry (src/domain/scan_to_map.cpp) and used in update_acc_term_after_window (src/application/run_odometry.cpp).

In one sentence Every surface a window's points are matched to pins the motion only across that surface, along its normal. Adding up n·nᵀ over all matched normals gives a 3 × 3 matrix whose weakest direction is the one in which the LiDAR can't see the motion. When that direction is much weaker than the strongest one, the window is degenerate.

1. The idea: a surface only fixes motion along its normal

The scan-to-map optimization moves each node until its points lie on the map's surfaces. A point on a wall tells the optimizer how far the sensor is from the wall, but nothing about where along the wall it is: sliding the sensor parallel to the wall leaves the point on the wall. So each matched point constrains the translation only along the surface normal n of the map cell it hits.

2. The matrix

For one window, optimize_lidar_odometry adds up, over every point matched to a fine-grid (0.15 m) cell with a valid normal:

I = Σ n · nᵀ        (3 × 3, world frame; n = the cell's unit surface normal)

Each term adds strength only along its own normal. The sum tells how strongly each direction is constrained, and its eigen-decomposition gives the answer directly:

Eigenvalue / eigenvectorMeaning
largest eigenvalueroughly how many points pin the best-constrained direction
smallest eigenvalue λminroughly how many pin the worst one
eigenvector of λminthe weakest direction: where the LiDAR is (nearly) blind
ratio = λmin / λmax0 = blind in one direction, 1 = equally constrained everywhere

Only the direction of each normal counts, not the distance or the NDT weight. That is deliberate. The NDT information itself (the inverse covariances) looks strong even in a corridor, because the small Gaussians also hold points in place along a flat wall, so its ratio doesn't reveal the blindness.

3. How dMapper uses it

Walking produces only about 0.2 m/s² of acceleration, while a 1° tilt error already leaves 0.17 m/s² of gravity in the accelerometer. So the accelerometer is a poor position sensor, and it is used only where the LiDAR is blind ([imu_translation]):

  1. After each window, the ratio of its information is computed. Below imu_degenerate_information_ratio (0.3), the window counts as degenerate.
  2. The next window then switches on the accelerometer term (the measured change of velocity between nodes), and loosens the translation prior from 1e-4 m to imu_degenerate_translation_sigma_m (3e-4 m), so the accelerometer can actually move the nodes along the blind direction.
  3. After imu_degenerate_max_duration_s (15 s) in one degenerate stretch, the term switches off again, because the accelerometer error grows with time.
  4. Outside degenerate stretches, the gravity vector and an acceleration offset are calibrated from the LiDAR trajectory, so they are ready for the next blind stretch.

This needs use_imu_acceleration_term = true and imu_acceleration_only_when_degenerate = true; both are on in settings/lidar-vertical.toml.

4. Reading the log

With the gating on, every window prints one line:

lidar translation information window 123: ratio 0.2140 weakest direction 0.98 0.17 0.02 | accelerometer term active
  • ratio below 0.3: this window is degenerate, so the accelerometer term will be active in the next window.
  • weakest direction: the unit vector (world x, y, z) the LiDAR can't see well; here almost x, i.e. along a corridor running in x. A vertical weakest direction (0 0 1) typically means a stairwell, or a space where floor and ceiling are out of view.
  • | accelerometer term active: the term was active in this window (decided by the previous one).

At the end the console prints how many windows used the term: accelerometer term: active in N of M windows.

5. Known weakness: the cell normals (found 2026-10-04)

The information is only as good as the map's normals, and many of them are wrong The synthetic scan-to-map test (tests/scan_to_map_test.cpp, change scan-to-map-test) put a window in a 30 m corridor. The information came out strongest along the corridor (925 along x against 155 across), the opposite of the truth.

The cause is in how the map cells are built (update_rgd, src/domain/ndt_map.cpp):

  • A cell's normal is computed once, when its third point arrives, from those 3 points plus the initial isotropic 3 cm covariance, and it is never updated. If the first 3 points lie roughly on a line, the normal can point anywhere in the plane the line leaves open. That is common, because points arrive in scan order, along the scan rings.
  • Later points update the covariance only if they pass the view-point test against that normal, so a wrong normal also leaves the covariance poorly estimated.
  • A cell's mean is its first point, and it is never updated either.

On real data the ratio is therefore noisy. Degenerate stretches were still detected on b1 (0.16–0.30 against 0.36 and more in rooms), but the wrong normals eat into that margin, and the reported weakest direction can be off. The same wrong normals make the view-point check in the scan-to-map optimization skip good cells.

Status (2026-10-04): a fix exists, but it is off. Change ndt-bucket-statistics added exact cell statistics behind the setting use_exact_ndt_bucket_statistics ([lidar_odometry_control], default false):

  • the mean and covariance are running statistics of all of a cell's points, with the 3 cm covariance as a prior worth one point;
  • the normal is recomputed from the covariance at the end of each map update;
  • a cell whose two smallest spreads differ by less than 3× gets no normal. Its points lie on one line or one cross-section, so no surface is defined.

With correct normals, the ratios on real data are much lower, and the threshold of 0.3, tuned on the wrong normals, then marks most of recording 44 as degenerate and switches the accelerometer term on in 411 windows. The map came out less sharp, but that mixes the new statistics with the much more frequent accelerometer term. The switch stays off for now (decision of 2026-10-04). The next step would be a run with imu_degenerate_information_ratio lowered until about as many windows count as degenerate as before (roughly 0.1–0.15). The test suite already runs on the exact statistics, so the corridor test is a normal passing test.
